# Finding Your Lane — Scene Research, August 2026

*Web-researched August 2026, cross-referenced against your library, your
influences, and your tools. Lanes shift — re-run this analysis every few
months (`docs/HOW-WE-MAKE-MUSIC.md` explains the framework).*

## What the scene data says right now

**Exploding (with numbers):** UK Garage / speed garage is the breakout
story — up 31% year-over-year in plays, +625% in Splice sample demand,
living at **130–140 BPM** (Sammy Virji, Interplanetary Criminal,
KETTAMA). Afro house (+778% Splice, "Sound of the Year" per MIDiA) and
melodic techno (Anyma's 100k-ticket Sphere run) are the other giants,
plus amapiano and Brazilian funk going institutional.

**The big undercurrent:** multiple sources flag *listener fatigue with
processed, algorithmic-sounding production* and rising demand for
"organic" sounds — real instruments, swing, soul, human imperfection.

**Bass music specifically:** dubstep has split into a polished
festival mainstream and a darker underground; midtempo (REZZ-lane) and
DnB keep expanding. Nobody covers riddim/tearout as "rising" anymore —
that scene is deep in its texture-glut phase: thousands of producers,
same Serum growls, same drop grammar. (Not your lane anyway — noted and
agreed.)

**The June 2026 plot twist:** GRiZ came back. After an 18-month hiatus,
*Future Funk Volume 1* (June 5) revived sax-driven, hip-hop-inflected
funk bass and pushed it "into heavier, more experimental territory"
(collabs: Big Gigantic, ProbCause, Effin). The lane you fell in love
with — the Weekend flip — just became a *rising current* again, and
it's young enough that the second wave hasn't formed yet.

## What you bring (the asset check)

- **Library:** 45k sounds, heavily bass-armed, all-minor top keys,
  tempo peaks at **110–129 and 140–159** — a perfect two-lane garage
  door onto funk-bass (110s) and the 140 continuum (dubstep AND speed
  garage).
- **Influences (corrected 2026-08-15):** a coherent triangle —
  **soul/funk** (GRiZ, first love), **heavy-but-musical bass**
  (Bassnectar above all, Subtronics, Peekaboo, Getter, Tape B), and
  **texture/beauty** (Flume, ODESZA, Kasbo, Whethan). Not riddim, not
  tearout. Bassnectar is the north star: one evolving signature sound
  and a fiercely dedicated fan base built on eclectic, mostly-original
  sets.
- **Tools:** a talky-bass factory (Serum 2 + formant tricks + AlterBoy),
  a huge Waves *analog-character* arsenal (Abbey Road tape, plates,
  consoles — exactly the "organic" texture the market is hungry for),
  and remake project files for REZZ, Hypnocurrency, and half the modern
  bass canon sitting in your MUSIC folder.
- **Edge:** a dissection system nobody else has. You can card and
  deconstruct any frontier track in minutes.

## Where NOT to plant your flag (head-on)

Riddim/tearout as-is (texture glut — enter only with a genuinely alien
sound), festival melodic techno (rising but instantly crowded, and not
your library), drift phonk (a content-farm economy, not a producer
scene).

## Three candidate lanes

**Lane A — The Funk-Bass Second Wave** *(ride a rising current, early)*
GRiZ just reopened funk-bass and is pushing it heavier — but one artist
isn't a scene; second waves are where new names get made. Your library's
110–129 peak, your Waves analog palette, and your flip project are
already pointed here. Sound: live-feeling funk grooves + modern heavy
bass design + hip-hop bounce. Card first: GRiZ "BBFL" + 2 more from
*Future Funk Vol. 1*, Big Gigantic, one classic funk break.

**Lane B — Textural 140: The Bassnectar Continuum** *(heavy AND
beautiful)* The dubby, spacious side of 140 is in open revival — Tape B
is its breakout proof — and it's the direct descendant of what
Bassnectar perfected: bass with real weight that stays *musical*,
omnivorous with samples, more texture than aggression. Your Flume /
ODESZA / Kasbo ear is the differentiator here: almost nobody in heavy
140 brings that grade of harmonic and textural beauty. Your library's
140–159 peak and Getter stem packs feed it directly. Sound: huge warm
subs, dub space and delay, painterly textures over the drop, melodic
interludes that earn the weight. Card first: Tape B ×2, one Bassnectar
era-defining cut, Peekaboo, one melodic Subtronics or Flume × bass
crossover. (UKG's exploding 2-step swing — +625% and rising — stays
available as *seasoning*: one swung groove per set, not the identity.)

**Lane C — The Spider-Gwen Alter-Ego** *(dark cinematic midtempo)*
Your Self Love remix instinct: mysterious, cinematic, mid-tempo.
REZZ-lane is expanding and less gluttted than dubstep, and you own
remake projects for its canon (Hex, Vortex, Hypnocurrency). Best held
as the alter-ego outlet — moody B-sides that teach you tension and
restraint while lanes A/B build your name.

## The recommendation

**Primary: A×B — soulful heavy bass.** Not either lane alone; the
braid. Funk groove and hip-hop bounce (GRiZ) fused with dubby,
textural 140 weight (Bassnectar → Tape B) and finished with
Flume/ODESZA-grade beauty in the sound design. Two market currents
back it — the funk-bass revival and the organic-texture demand — and
your library covers both of its tempo homes (110s and 140s). GRiZ
brought funk to dubstep; Bassnectar proved heavy could be a whole
musical world with a devoted congregation. The seat marked *"heavy bass
with soul, swing, and genuinely beautiful texture"* is, per this
research, not fully occupied by anyone of the new generation.

**Keep C as the alter-ego.** Different name later if it grows.

**The fan-base model (the Bassnectar playbook, minus nothing):** a
dedicated fan base is built less by any single sound than by *ritual* —
sets that are events, mostly original material and personal edits,
constant small releases, and direct community. Your all-originals goal
is exactly this playbook, and it's where the automation genuinely
compounds: flips and edits (mashup_match + stems) are how a young
catalog fills a full set before you have 40 originals.

## Validate it in four weeks (evidence, not vibes)

1. **Week 1:** Card the 5 Lane-A + 5 Lane-B tracks above. Dissect two.
2. **Weeks 2–3:** One sketch per lane from the recipes. Same effort each.
3. **Week 4:** Bounce, compare against cards, and ship both roughs to
   SoundCloud. Then read three signals: which gaps closed faster, which
   got responses, and — the most honest datum — **which one you
   couldn't stop working on.** Joy is data; the lane you'll win is the
   one you'll outwork everyone in.

Re-run the scene research after ~10 shipped tracks. Lanes are chosen,
then earned.
